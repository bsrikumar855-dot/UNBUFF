# Monopoly lens
Score: 3 | Vote: PASS

## What is strong
- The small market is real and narrow: pre-final and final year Indian engineering students facing campus placements. That is a defined group with a fixed calendar and a hard deadline, which is where a dominant share can start. (Target market is an assumption; the founder has not written it down.)
- There is one genuine candidate for a secret: placement preparation built only on verified evidence, where self-reported or stand-in-judge results never count. A rule like that is hard for a bluffing-friendly competitor to copy without hurting its own numbers.
- A student's verified record (audit results, judged code, quiz scores, mentor memory) could become a switching cost over months. That is the only route to something durable here.
- Costs are low today because everything runs on free tiers, so little is lost by learning.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. No proprietary edge ten times better. Interview audit, DSA practice, DBMS quizzes, an AI mentor, a LaTeX resume builder and job matching are each an incremental version of something that already exists. The mentor and grading sit on a rented Gemini model that any rival can also rent. What stops a copycat in month six? Nothing in the brief.
2. The "verified" claim is not yet true. Code judging needs an execution service that is not deployed, the company-wise question list has no licence, and the resume compiler is development-only. The one differentiator is the part that does not run.
3. No distribution and no way to charge. Channels (college groups, YouTube/Instagram, word of mouth) are not started, there is no payment processor or legal entity, and the business model is undecided. A good product with no path to its first 100 customers is not yet a business. Each paid user also carries a per-message AI cost, and the price (INR 299 a month) is untested.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Which one of these features, alone, would a student pay for, and why can no one else supply it?
- What is the single small market you own first: one college, one branch, one placement cohort?
- What do you know about how students are screened that others do not? That is the secret; I do not see it yet.
- How do the first 100 students arrive, and what does each one cost?
- Does a student's verified record become worth more over time, and could they take it elsewhere?
- What happens to margins when the AI cost per student grows with usage?
- Who else sells placement preparation to the same buyer, and what do they do better today? (Competitor list: open question, none provided.)

## If FUND IF: the conditions
Not applicable (vote is PASS). To reconsider: show one wedge with a small, named student cohort using it, with real verified (not stand-in) code judging live, evidence of a switching cost from accumulated records, and a measured cost to serve one paying student. Market size and willingness to pay are open questions nobody has yet.
